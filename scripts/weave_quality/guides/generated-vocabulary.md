id: generated-vocabulary
violation_type: prose:generated-vocabulary
summary: Stock words common in generated text stand in for the claim the sentence should make.
---

## Why

Words such as `delve`, `leverage`, `robust`, `seamless` and `comprehensive` appear far more often in
generated text than in technical writing, and readers have learned to notice them. Each one replaces
a specific statement with a general approval. `A robust parser` tells the reader nothing they can
check; `a parser that rejects truncated input and reports the byte offset` tells them what it does.
The word costs the document credibility out of proportion to its length.

## Corrective move

Ask what property the word was standing for, and write that property instead: the input it handles,
the failure it prevents, the measurement it meets, the number of cases it covers. If no property
comes to mind, the word was decoration and the sentence reads better without it. A verb such as
`delve into` usually reduces to the plain verb for what happened: `read`, `compare`, `measure`,
`trace`.

## Expected outcome

Each sentence makes a claim a reader could test or dispute. Adjectives that remain carry
information, such as a size, a rate, a limit or a condition.

## Avoid

Swapping the flagged word for a synonym that says just as little (`powerful`, `cutting-edge`,
`effortless`). The finding is about the missing claim, and a different empty word keeps it missing.
