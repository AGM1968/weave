---
id: unearned-significance
violation_type: prose:unearned-significance
summary: Text announces that something is important instead of stating the thing.
---

## Why

A heading such as `The cache, and why it matters` or a sentence opening `This matters because`
promises importance before delivering content. The promise does no work: a reader learns whether
something matters from what follows, not from being told in advance. Scaffolding of this kind also
makes every section sound equally urgent, which leaves the reader with no way to tell which ones
are.

## Corrective move

Name the content directly. A heading names the subject or the relationship it covers
(`Cache invalidation on schema change`), not the promise that it will be interesting. In a sentence,
delete the announcement and start with the fact it was announcing; if the importance is itself the
point, state the consequence it rests on, such as the failure it prevents or the cost it saves.

## Expected outcome

Headings work as an index: each says what is under it. Sentences open with their claims, and any
statement of importance is backed by a stated consequence.

## Avoid

Moving the announcement somewhere less visible (into a bold lead-in, or a different phrasing of the
same promise) instead of removing it. The finding is about announcing instead of stating, wherever
the announcement sits.
