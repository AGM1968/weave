---
id: filler
violation_type: prose:filler
summary: A long phrase stands where a short exact equivalent would say the same thing.
---

## Why

Phrases such as `due to the fact that`, `at this point in time` and `in order to` add words without
adding meaning. Each one makes the reader hold more of the sentence before reaching its content, and
a document full of them reads as padded even when every claim in it is sound. Hedging phrases of the
same kind weaken a statement without saying what the doubt is.

## Corrective move

Replace the phrase with its direct form: `because` for `due to the fact that`, `now` for
`at this point in time`, `to` for `in order to`. If a hedge expresses an uncertainty the reader
needs, replace it with the uncertainty itself, such as the condition under which the statement fails
or the measurement that is missing. If it expresses none, delete it.

## Expected outcome

Sentences are as long as their content requires. A reader reaches the subject and the verb without
passing through phrases that could be removed without changing the meaning.

## Avoid

Shortening a sentence by dropping a qualification the claim depends on. A condition, a limit or a
measured range is content, not filler, and the corrective move keeps it.
