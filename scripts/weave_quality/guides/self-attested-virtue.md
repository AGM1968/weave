---
id: self-attested-virtue
violation_type: prose:self-attested-virtue
summary: The text vouches for its own rigour or verification instead of showing the evidence.
---

## Why

Words that praise the writing or the work (`candid`, `thorough`, `carefully verified`,
`validated successfully`) ask the reader to accept a quality on the author's word. A reader cannot
check an adjective. Worse, the claim is easiest to make exactly where the evidence is thinnest.
Readers learn to discount it, and the document loses trust it may have earned elsewhere.

## Corrective move

Replace the virtue with the property or evidence it stood for. Instead of saying a result was
verified, state what was checked, by what procedure, and against what threshold:
`the 36 cases fail against the unfixed code and pass against the fixed code`. Instead of describing
the analysis as `candid`, state the limitation it reports. Where the word meant a statistical or
logical property, name that property: unbiased, bounded, measured, unverified.

## Expected outcome

Claims of quality are carried by numbers, procedures and stated limits that a reader can inspect.
Nothing in the document depends on the reader trusting how the author describes their own care.

## Avoid

Replacing one self-description with another (`rigorous` for `thorough`), or moving the claim into a
heading. Avoid also deleting the evidence along with the adjective; the evidence is what the reader
needed.
