summary: One punctuation habit is dense enough to read as a tic and to hide the sentence structure.
---

## Why

The em dash is useful for one interruption in a sentence. Used several times in a paragraph, it
turns the paragraph into a chain of asides, and the reader can no longer tell which clause is the
claim and which is commentary. The same applies to any punctuation mark used out of habit rather
than for the relationship it signals.

## Corrective move

Keep the strongest interruption, if any, and express the other relationships with the punctuation or
words that name them. A separate point becomes its own sentence. An example or a qualification goes
in parentheses or after a comma. A cause, contrast or consequence gets an explicit connective
(`because`, `but`, `which means`), which tells the reader what the relationship is instead of only
that one exists.

## Expected outcome

Each paragraph reads with a clear main line. Interruptions are rare enough that each one is noticed,
and the relationships between clauses are stated rather than implied by punctuation.

## Avoid

Substituting another mark for the same habit, such as replacing every em dash with a semicolon or a
pair of commas. The density is the finding; changing the character keeps the chain of asides.
