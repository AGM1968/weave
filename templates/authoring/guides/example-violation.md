## Why

Say what the construction actually costs, in terms of what goes wrong later and
for whom. A reader who already agrees the pattern is bad learns nothing from
being told it is bad again; a reader who does not agree is not persuaded by the
detector's existence. Name the failure the pattern produces.

## Corrective move

Say what to write instead, concretely enough to act on without re-reading the
rule. One move, stated once. If the correct move depends on which form of the
violation is present, that is what a declared section below is for.

## Expected outcome

Say what the code or prose looks like once the move has been made, so the
reader can tell whether they are finished.

## Avoid

Name the ways a detector can be satisfied without the fault being fixed. This
section matters more than its position suggests: the reader is often an agent,
and an agent handed a pattern to avoid will avoid the pattern rather than the
problem unless told the difference.

## A second form, where the same fault appears inverted

A declared section, admitted only because the frontmatter above names it. Use
these for the sub-forms of one violation type that need separate treatment --
the case where the same underlying fault presents differently enough that one
corrective move cannot cover both.

Delete the declaration when you delete the section: a name declared and never
used is an error, not a leftover.

## Exemplars from the corpus

The other reason to declare a section. A guide that can point at real instances
in this repository teaches faster than one that describes them, and the
instances are repository-specific, which is exactly why this is a local tier
rather than a heading Weave ships.
